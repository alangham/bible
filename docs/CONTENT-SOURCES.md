## How the introductions were written

- Each source page was downloaded once, on 2026-11-13. The Easton and Wikipedia addresses below are fixed revisions (`oldid`).
- Each fact is in my own words. A script checked that no run of 6 words in an introduction is the same as a run in the ESV Global Study Bible text or the Wikipedia text of that book.
- "Single source" marks a fact that only one of the two traditional sources states.
- "What it is" was compared with the first and the last chapter of the Berean Standard Bible text, as a minimum.
- Each introduction has `review: "draft"`. Only Arthur changes it to `"approved"`.

## Book introductions

### Genesis (`genesis`)

Review: draft. Fixed source pages: ESV https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-genesis/ . Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Genesis&oldid=1385020338 .

- Author: Moses, by tradition. Many scholars think several writers and editors produced it long after Moses.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FGenesis&oldid=2961148. States: Names Moses as the author of Genesis, perhaps using older documents and traditions.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FPentateuch&oldid=14372282. States: Holds that Moses wrote the whole Pentateuch.
  - ESV Global Study Bible, https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-genesis/. States: Says tradition names Moses as author of Genesis and of the other four books of the Pentateuch.
  - Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Genesis&oldid=1385020338, section "Composition". States: Says modern scholars hold that several authors and editors produced Genesis much later than Moses. Cited scholar: Simon Oliver, Creation, Bloomsbury (2017), p. 11.
- Written: By tradition, in the time of Moses, for the Israelites he led through the wilderness. Most scholars now date its final shape after the exile in Babylon, perhaps the 5th century BC.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FGenesis&oldid=2961148. States: Names Moses as the author. Gives no year and no place of writing.
  - ESV Global Study Bible, https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-genesis/. States: Says the first readers were the Israelites whom Moses led through the wilderness. Gives no year of writing. Single source.
  - Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Genesis&oldid=1385020338, section "Composition". States: Says that in this century most scholars put the finished book after the exile in Babylon, perhaps in the 5th century BCE. Cited scholar: Simon Oliver, Creation, Bloomsbury (2017), pp. 11 to 12.
- What it is: A narrative that begins with creation, then tells of the flood and the families of Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and Joseph. It ends with Joseph's death in Egypt, where Jacob's family now lives.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FGenesis&oldid=2961148. States: Part one, chapters 1 to 11, is a general history of mankind. Part two, chapters 12 to 50, is the early history of Israel to the death of Joseph. Names Adam, Noah, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.
  - ESV Global Study Bible, https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-genesis/. States: Mentions creation and the flood. The outline covers primeval history and the patriarchs to the move of Jacob's family to Egypt.
  - Berean Standard Bible. States: Chapter 1 tells how God created the heavens, the earth, and mankind. Chapter 50 tells of the burial of Jacob in Canaan, and says Joseph's household stayed in Egypt, where Joseph died at 110.
- Not verified:
  - Date and place of writing: neither Easton nor the ESV GSB gives a year or a place of writing for Genesis. The Written line therefore gives no year for the traditional view.

### Exodus (`exodus`)

Review: draft. Fixed source pages: ESV https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-exodus/ . Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Exodus&oldid=1383653829 .

- Author: Moses, by tradition, with some details added later. By the late 1800s, many scholars had given up this view.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FExodus%2C_Book_of&oldid=721436. States: Gives Moses as author of Exodus, as of the other books of the Pentateuch.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FPentateuch&oldid=14372282. States: Allows that the writing of Moses was revised by later inspired writers.
  - ESV Global Study Bible, https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-exodus/. States: Says Moses wrote Exodus for the most part, and that some details were added and some wording updated later.
  - Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Exodus&oldid=1383653829, section "Composition: Authorship". States: Says Jewish and Christian tradition credits Moses, but scholars had given up that idea before 1900. Cited scholar: Carol Meyers, Exodus, Cambridge University Press (2005), p. 16.
- Written: By tradition, in the time of Moses, after Israel left Egypt. Some scholars date its formation to the 5th or 4th century BC.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FExodus%2C_Book_of&oldid=721436. States: Ascribes the book to Moses. Says the book covers the time from the death of Joseph to the setting up of the tabernacle. Gives no date of writing.
  - ESV Global Study Bible, https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-exodus/. States: Says Moses wrote it. Dates the exodus events, not the writing.
  - Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Exodus&oldid=1383653829, section "Composition: Authorship". States: Says the formation of the Pentateuch, Exodus included, is dated to the 5th to 4th centuries BC. Cited scholar: Liane M. Feldman, The Consuming Fire: The Complete Priestly Source, from Creation to the Promised Land, University of California Press (2023), pp. 6 to 7.
- What it is: A narrative of Israel's escape from slavery in Egypt and its journey to Mount Sinai. There God gives the law, and Moses sets up the tabernacle.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FExodus%2C_Book_of&oldid=721436. States: Lists the contents: Israel's growth in Egypt, the departure, the journey to Sinai, the giving of the law, and the setting up of the tabernacle.
  - Berean Standard Bible. States: Chapter 1 tells how a new king of Egypt forced the Israelites into hard labor. Chapter 40 tells how Moses set up the tabernacle and the glory of the LORD filled it.
- Not verified:
  - Date and place of writing: neither Easton nor the ESV GSB gives a date or a place of writing for Exodus.
  - The ESV GSB dates the exodus events, not the writing. It gives c. 1446 BC as a common view and c. 1260 BC as the view of some scholars. I did not use either year.

### Leviticus (`leviticus`)

Review: draft. Fixed source pages: ESV https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-leviticus/ . Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Leviticus&oldid=1383765592 .

- Author: Moses, by tradition. Most scholars assign chapters 1 to 26 to two linked schools of priestly writers.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FLeviticus&oldid=736142. States: Calls Leviticus the third book of Moses.
  - ESV Global Study Bible, https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-leviticus/. States: Treats Moses as the main author and the origin of the book.
  - Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Leviticus&oldid=1383765592, section "Composition". States: Says most scholars give chapters 1 to 16 and 17 to 26, known as the Priestly code and the Holiness code, to two linked schools. Cited scholar: Walter J. Houston, Leviticus, in Eerdmans Bible Commentary (2003), p. 102.
- Written: By tradition, the laws were given at Mount Sinai in the second year after the exodus. Most scholars place the final form of the first five books in the Persian period, 538 to 332 BC.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FLeviticus&oldid=736142. States: Says the laws were all given within one month, the first month of the second year after the exodus. Single source.
  - ESV Global Study Bible, https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-leviticus/. States: Says the book details the relationship set up on Mount Sinai.
  - Berean Standard Bible. States: Chapter 27 verse 34 says the LORD gave these commandments to Moses on Mount Sinai.
  - Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Leviticus&oldid=1383765592, section "Composition". States: Says the majority of scholars hold that the Pentateuch reached its final form in the Persian period, 538 to 332 BC. Cited scholar: Carol Ann Newsom, The Self as Symbolic Space: Constructing Identity and Community at Qumran, Brill (2004), p. 26.
- What it is: A book of laws on offerings, priests, purity, and feasts, given by God to Moses. The setting is the Tent of Meeting at Mount Sinai.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FLeviticus&oldid=736142. States: Lists laws on sacrifices, priestly duties, purity, separation from other nations, festivals, and vows.
  - Berean Standard Bible. States: In chapter 1 the LORD calls Moses and speaks about burnt offerings at the Tent of Meeting. Chapter 27 ends with the commandments given to Moses on Mount Sinai.
- Not verified:
  - Date of writing: neither Easton nor the ESV GSB gives a date of writing. Easton dates the giving of the laws, not the writing. The Written line reports when the laws were given.
  - Place of writing: neither source gives one. Mount Sinai is the place where the laws were given, from the ESV GSB and the BSB. Easton's entry does not name Sinai.

### Numbers (`numbers`)

Review: draft. Fixed source pages: ESV https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-numbers/ . Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Numbers&oldid=1384977761 .

- Author: Moses, by tradition. Some scholars see it as an older text reworked by priestly editors.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FNumbers%2C_Book_of&oldid=759898. States: Says the book, like the rest of the Pentateuch, shows evidence of being written by Moses.
  - ESV Global Study Bible, https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-numbers/. States: Treats Moses as the main author and the origin of the book.
  - Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Numbers&oldid=1384977761, section "Composition". States: Says Numbers is a Priestly redaction of a non-Priestly original, as part of the outline of the source hypothesis. Cited scholar: John J. McDermott, Reading the Pentateuch: A Historical Introduction, Pauline Press (2002), p. 21.
- Written: By tradition, in the lifetime of Moses. Most scholars date the present form of the first five books after about 520 BC.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FNumbers%2C_Book_of&oldid=759898. States: Names Moses as the writer. Says the book covers about 38 years and 10 months, from the second year after the exodus to the fortieth year.
  - ESV Global Study Bible, https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-numbers/. States: Names Moses as author. Says the book sums up about 40 years of Israel's history.
  - Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Numbers&oldid=1384977761, section "Composition". States: Says most modern scholars place the Torah in its present form after about 520 BC. Cited scholar: Peter Enns; Israel Finkelstein and Neil Asher Silberman; John J. McDermott, The Evolution of Adam (2012), p. 5; The Bible Unearthed (2002), pp. 68 to 69; Reading the Pentateuch (2002), p. 21.
- What it is: A narrative with laws, following Israel through the wilderness from Mount Sinai to the plains of Moab. It takes its name from two counts of the people.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FNumbers%2C_Book_of&oldid=759898. States: Says the name comes from the counting of the people at Sinai, chapters 1 to 4, and on the plain of Moab, chapter 26. Describes the route through the wilderness.
  - ESV Global Study Bible, https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-numbers/. States: Says the book follows Israel from Sinai up to the edge of the land promised to them.
  - Berean Standard Bible. States: Chapter 1 is a census in the Wilderness of Sinai. Chapter 36 ends with commandments given through Moses on the plains of Moab by the Jordan.
- Not verified:
  - Date and place of writing: neither Easton nor the ESV GSB gives a year or a place of writing. The Written line says only that Moses wrote it in his lifetime.

### Deuteronomy (`deuteronomy`)

Review: draft. Fixed source pages: ESV https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-deuteronomy/ . Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Deuteronomy&oldid=1381459516 .

- Author: Moses, by tradition, apart from the account of his death. Most scholars reject the view that Moses wrote it.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FDeuteronomy&oldid=12568368. States: Names Moses as the author. Says the account of his death and burial was written by another hand, probably Joshua. Single source.
  - ESV Global Study Bible, https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-deuteronomy/. States: Calls the book sermons preached by Moses. Does not state who wrote the book.
  - Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Deuteronomy&oldid=1381459516, section "Composition: Composition history". States: Says almost every secular scholar, and most Christian scholars and Jewish scholars too, deny that Moses wrote Deuteronomy. Cited scholar: Jeffrey Stackert, Deuteronomy and the Pentateuch, Yale University Press (2022), p. 136.
- Written: By tradition, Moses gave these speeches in Moab, shortly before he died. Most scholars date the book from the 7th to the 5th century BC.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FDeuteronomy&oldid=12568368. States: Says the speeches were given to all Israel on the plains of Moab, in the eleventh month of the last year of the wanderings, a short time before Moses died.
  - ESV Global Study Bible, https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-deuteronomy/. States: Says Moses gave these sermons to Israel just before he died, when the people stood east of the promised land.
  - Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Deuteronomy&oldid=1381459516, section "Composition: Composition history". States: Says those scholars put the book far later, in the 7th to 5th centuries BCE. Cited scholar: Jeffrey Stackert, Deuteronomy and the Pentateuch, Yale University Press (2022), p. 136.
- What it is: Speeches of Moses that retell Israel's journey and restate the law before the people enter Canaan. It ends with the death of Moses.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FDeuteronomy&oldid=12568368. States: Says the book is mainly three speeches of Moses that review the 40 years and restate the law, then a song, blessings, and the account of his death.
  - ESV Global Study Bible, https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-deuteronomy/. States: Says the book contains Moses' last sermons, urging Israel to keep the law when they enter the land.
  - Berean Standard Bible. States: Chapter 1 says these are the words Moses spoke to Israel east of the Jordan in Moab. Chapter 34 tells of the death of Moses in Moab.
- Not verified:
  - Author: the ESV GSB introduction to Deuteronomy does not state who wrote the book. The author line rests on Easton only.
  - Date: Easton gives the eleventh month of the last year of the wanderings, and the ESV GSB gives shortly before the death of Moses. Neither gives a year, so the Written line has no year for the traditional view.

### Joshua (`joshua`)

Review: draft. Fixed source pages: ESV https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-joshua/ . Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Joshua&oldid=1382152172 .

- Author: Joshua, by Jewish tradition, except the last verses. The book itself does not say that he wrote it.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FJoshua%2C_Book_of&oldid=809141. States: Accepts the Jewish tradition that Joshua wrote the book, except the last verses, 24:29 to 33, added by another hand. Single source.
  - ESV Global Study Bible, https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-joshua/. States: Says the book mentions Joshua writing, at 8:32 and 24:26, but does not claim that he wrote the book. Single source.
- Written: The date is uncertain. Most scholars agree it was first written at King Josiah's court, late in the 7th century BC.
  - ESV Global Study Bible, https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-joshua/. States: Says the book may have been finished in the exile, after 587 BC, though work on it likely started long before. So the date is not settled. Single source.
  - Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Joshua&oldid=1382152172, section "Composition: Authorship and date". States: Says scholars now broadly agree the book belongs to the Deuteronomistic history, first drafted under King Josiah near the end of the 7th century BC and heavily reworked in the 6th. Cited scholar: K. Lawson Younger Jr; Albert de Pury and Thomas Romer, Joshua, in Eerdmans Commentary on the Bible (2003), p. 174; Deuteronomistic Historiography, in Israel Constructs its History (2000), p. 63.
- What it is: A narrative of how Israel, led by Joshua, takes Canaan and divides the land among the tribes. It ends with Joshua's farewell and death.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FJoshua%2C_Book_of&oldid=809141. States: Says the book covers the conquest, the allotment of the land to the tribes, and the farewell speeches and death of Joshua.
  - Berean Standard Bible. States: Chapter 1 tells how the LORD sends Joshua to cross the Jordan into the land. Chapter 24 tells of the covenant at Shechem and the death of Joshua.
- Not verified:
  - Author: Easton accepts the Jewish tradition that Joshua wrote the book, except 24:29 to 33. The ESV GSB names no author and says the book does not claim that Joshua wrote it.
  - Date: Easton's view places the writing in the lifetime of Joshua but gives no year. The ESV GSB says the final writing may date from the exile after 587 BC and began much earlier. I wrote only that the date is uncertain.
  - Place of writing: neither source gives one.

### Judges (`judges`)

Review: draft. Fixed source pages: ESV https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-judges/ . Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Judges&oldid=1383106058 .

- Author: Samuel, in one older view. The Bible itself names no author.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FJudges%2C_Book_of&oldid=736128. States: Says the author was most probably Samuel, from internal evidence. Single source.
  - ESV Global Study Bible, https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-judges/. States: Says no author of the book is named anywhere in Scripture. Single source.
- Written: Probably no later than the reign of King David, about 1000 BC. Martin Noth dated the larger history that includes it to the 6th century BC.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FJudges%2C_Book_of&oldid=736128. States: Says the book was probably written in the reign of Saul or at the very start of the reign of David.
  - ESV Global Study Bible, https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-judges/. States: Says the bulk of the book was probably done by David's time, 1010 to 970 BC.
  - Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Judges&oldid=1383106058, section "Composition: The Deuteronomistic History". States: Noth dated the Deuteronomy to Kings history to the early exile, 6th century BCE. Since the later 20th century most scholars accept his view that these books form one work. Cited scholar: Martin Noth, as reported by John H. Walton; Gary Knoppers, Walton, The Deuteronomistic History, in A Survey of the Old Testament, Zondervan (2009), pp. 169 to 170; Knoppers, Introduction, in Reconsidering Israel and Judah, Eisenbrauns (2000), p. 1.
- What it is: A narrative of Israel after the death of Joshua, when leaders called judges rescued the tribes from enemies. It ends in civil war, in days when Israel had no king.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FJudges%2C_Book_of&oldid=736128. States: Says the book tells how men called judges delivered and governed Israel, with an appendix on Dan and on the war against Benjamin.
  - Berean Standard Bible. States: Chapter 1 begins after the death of Joshua. Chapter 21 tells how the tribes found wives for Benjamin after the war. Its last verse says Israel had no king then.
- Not verified:
  - Author: Easton names Samuel as the most probable author, from internal evidence. Easton does not call this a tradition. The ESV GSB says Scripture names no author. I wrote both views without choosing.
  - Date: Easton gives the reign of Saul or the start of the reign of David. The ESV GSB says most of the book was written by the time of David. I wrote only the part that both support.
  - Place of writing: neither source gives one.
  - The scholars line gives Martin Noth's view by name. The article also reports a different view by Frank Moore Cross of a first version in the late 7th century BC.

### Ruth (`ruth`)

Review: draft. Fixed source pages: ESV https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-ruth/ . Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Ruth&oldid=1374486736 .

- Author: Samuel, by Jewish tradition. The book itself names no author.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FRuth%2C_Book_of&oldid=739870. States: Says the author was probably Samuel, according to Jewish tradition. Single source.
  - ESV Global Study Bible, https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-ruth/. States: Says no author is named. Single source.
  - Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Ruth&oldid=1374486736, section "Composition and genre". States: Says the text names no author and that tradition gives it to Samuel. Cited scholar: Robert L. Hubbard Jr; Mary Joan Winn Leith, The Book of Ruth, Eerdmans (1988), p. 23; Ruth, in The New Oxford Annotated Bible (2007), p. 391.
- Written: Not before David became king, about 1010 BC. Many scholars place it in the Persian period, 550 to 330 BC.
  - ESV Global Study Bible, https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-ruth/. States: Says the book names David and his ancestors, so it was written after he became king, c. 1010 BC. Single source.
  - Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Book_of_Ruth&oldid=1374486736, section "Composition and genre". States: Says a substantial number of scholars date the book to the Persian period, 550 to 330 BCE. Cited scholar: Lester L. Grabbe, The History of the Jews and Judaism in the Second Temple Period, Volume 1, Continuum (2004), p. 105.
- What it is: A narrative about the widow Naomi, Ruth from Moab, and Boaz of Bethlehem. It is set when the judges ruled and ends with the family line of David.
  - Easton's Bible Dictionary, https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Easton's_Bible_Dictionary_(1897)%2FRuth%2C_Book_of&oldid=739870. States: Says the book tells of Naomi going to Moab and coming back to Bethlehem with Ruth, the marriage of Boaz and Ruth, and the birth of Obed, ancestor of David.
  - Berean Standard Bible. States: Chapter 1 opens in the days when the judges ruled, with Naomi's family in Moab. Chapter 4 tells of Boaz marrying Ruth and ends with the family line from Perez to David.
- Not verified:
  - Author: Easton gives Samuel as the probable author by Jewish tradition. The ESV GSB names no author and does not mention Samuel.
  - Date: Easton gives no date of writing. The Written line rests on the ESV GSB only.
  - Place of writing: neither source gives one.

### 1 Samuel (`1-samuel`)

Review: draft. Fixed source pages: ESV https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-1-2-samuel/ . Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Books_of_Samuel&oldid=1381292867 .
